# Interview Transcript: Sales Order Processing with Customer Down Payment (BKJ)
**Format:** Discovery interview, UX researcher × business process expert
**Participants:** Uma Xavier (UX researcher) · Bex Pryor (Business Process Expert)
**Process scope:** S/4HANA: Sales Order Processing with Customer Down Payment (BKJ)
**Duration (estimated):** ~30 minutes
**Note:** Lightly edited for readability. Speaker labels preserved as recorded.

---

**Uma:** Alright, so, thanks for making time. What I'd love to do today is just walk through this process from the beginning. Like you'd explain it to someone who understands sales but has never seen it in S/4HANA. Can you start by telling me what this is actually about, in plain terms?

**Bex:** Sure. The core idea is pretty simple. It's about selling something where the customer pays you a portion upfront, before you ship. A down payment. Sounds straightforward, but there are quite a few moving parts once you're in the system. The tricky bit is the billing; it's not just one document. You've got the down payment request first, then the actual invoice later. The system has to link those together and make sure the final invoice correctly deducts what was already paid.

**Uma:** Got it. Who typically triggers this? Like, what's the business situation where it comes up?

**Bex:** Usually higher-value orders, or with new customers where you want some financial security upfront. A sales rep gets an order and as part of the deal there's an agreement: you pay thirty percent now, we ship, then you pay the rest on delivery or net thirty, whatever the terms are. The billing plan is how that agreement gets captured in the system.

**Uma:** Okay, let's start there. The sales rep creates the order; walk me through what that looks like.

**Bex:** Right. The sales rep logs in and goes into the Manage Sales Orders app, or directly into VA01. They put in the sales document type, the sales org, distribution channel, division; that's all standard header stuff. Then the sold-to party, material, quantity. Nothing unusual so far.

The special thing is the item category. You need a specific one; CBAO for trading goods. That tells the system this item participates in a milestone billing plan. Wrong item category and you don't get the billing plan functionality at all. That matters.

**Uma:** And then?

**Bex:** Then you go into the billing plan for that item. Two lines to set up. First line is the down payment; you put in a billing date, the amount or percentage, and the date category 04, which is the down payment category. Second line is the final invoice; billing date and date category 02. The system calculates the remaining amount automatically. So if the order is a thousand euros and the down payment is three hundred, the final invoice line shows seven hundred.

**Uma:** Both set at the time of the sales order?

**Bex:** Yes, both defined in the order. That's what makes this a milestone billing plan; the whole billing schedule is agreed at the start and sits in the order. The system then uses those dates and categories to control what gets billed when.

**Uma:** Okay, and then there's something about a billing block?

**Bex:** Yeah, this is where it gets a bit nuanced. When you create the order with this billing plan, the down payment line can get a billing block set automatically; depends on the config. The block means the billing clerk can't process the down payment request until someone releases it. There's a checkpoint there; maybe a manager confirms the payment terms, or credit signs off. So after saving the order the sales rep needs to go back in and manually remove that billing block from the down payment line in the billing plan.

**Uma:** So that's a separate step; remove the block?

**Bex:** Exactly. A change to the sales order. Go in, navigate to the billing plan, find the down payment date line, delete the block, save. Now it's open for billing.

**Uma:** From a business standpoint, how do you know that step worked correctly?

**Bex:** The billing block column should be empty for the down payment line. The final invoice line should still have its block in place; that one stays until after goods issue. If both blocks are gone at this point, something's wrong. Only the down payment should be unblocked now.

**Uma:** Good. So now the billing clerk takes over?

**Bex:** Right. The billing clerk goes into the Create Billing Documents app and looks at the billing due list; that's a list of everything due for billing. They find the relevant sales order item, specifically the down payment line, select it, trigger billing. When it asks for billing type, they choose FAZ; that's the down payment request document type. Pick a billing date, create it, save. That creates a billing document, a down payment request, which goes out to the customer. Not an invoice yet in the traditional sense; it's a request for the advance payment.

**Uma:** Is that sent automatically, or is there a separate output step?

**Bex:** There's an output condition on the billing document. Standard setup generates a PDF; the billing clerk can preview it and it goes out via output management. For testing you'd typically just check the output condition is there and the preview looks right. Actually sending it out is more of an integration test; for functional purposes it's fine to just confirm the document is correct and the output type is set.

**Uma:** And what does "done right" look like at this point?

**Bex:** The billing document is created and posted. The financial posting is a debit to the down payment receivable account; something like account 21191000 in the demo system, but in a productive system, say PR1 or PRD, the actual account number will depend on your chart of accounts configuration, so treat that as an example value. What matters is the posting goes to the right account type; down payment receivable, not revenue. The billing document should show the correct amount, the correct customer, and reference back to the original sales order so the document flow is intact.

**Uma:** Document flow; that's the traceability thing?

**Bex:** Yes. One of the key things in this whole process is that every step is linked. Sales order to billing request to payment to delivery to final invoice. You should be able to open any document and navigate up or down the chain. If that chain is broken at any point; say a billing document doesn't reference the right sales order; that's a problem. Business perspective and audit perspective.

**Uma:** Alright. So the customer pays, and then what happens on your end?

**Bex:** The accounts receivable accountant posts the incoming payment. They use the Post Incoming Payments app. They enter the bank data, the amount the customer paid, then go looking for the open item; that's the FAZ billing document. There's a specific filter needed here. They need to look for special G/L transactions, because down payment requests are posted to a special G/L account, not normal receivables. Once they find it, they clear it. Customer has paid, the liability is settled, delivery can proceed.

**Uma:** Is that automatic, or does someone have to trigger the delivery release?

**Bex:** There's a config option where clearing the down payment automatically removes the delivery block from the sales order schedule lines. Whether that's set up or has to be done manually depends on the implementation. But either way, the business rule is firm: no delivery until the down payment is confirmed posted. That's a hard acceptance criterion. If you can create a delivery before the down payment is cleared, the control mechanism is broken.

**Uma:** Got it. So after payment, shipping takes over?

**Bex:** Yes. The shipping specialist creates the outbound delivery from the sales order; there's an app for that. They enter the shipping point and the planned date, find the sales order, trigger the delivery creation. The system checks stock availability. No stock, no delivery; that's a prerequisite.

**Uma:** And picking, is that part of this flow?

**Bex:** Yes, next step. The shipping specialist goes into the outbound delivery monitor, filters for items due for picking, finds the delivery, enters the picked quantity. That confirms the goods have been taken from storage and staged for shipping. Save the delivery. For batch-managed materials there's an extra step for the batch number, but for standard trading goods it's straightforward.

**Uma:** How does the shipping specialist find their delivery? In a real warehouse there'd be dozens of open ones.

**Bex:** In real life they'd filter by shipping point, date, maybe customer. In a test setup you're usually working with just your own delivery so it's easy. But you should still filter by shipping point in the test; that's how the app is designed. And you confirm your delivery shows up correctly under the "for picking" view. That's actually a subtle acceptance criterion; the delivery appears in the right worklist for the right role. If a shipping specialist can't see their delivery in the picking list, something in the delivery setup is off.

**Uma:** What does correct picking look like as an outcome?

**Bex:** Picked quantity matches delivery quantity. Delivery status updates to show picking complete. Batch assigned if relevant. After that, the delivery is ready for goods issue.

**Uma:** And then?

**Bex:** Post Goods Issue; that's where the system records the goods have physically left the warehouse. Back into the delivery monitor, filter for goods issue, select the delivery, post. This triggers an inventory reduction and a financial posting: cost of goods sold debited, inventory credited. From this point, revenue recognition is possible.

**Uma:** Is there a confirmation the shipping specialist sees?

**Bex:** Yes; success message and the delivery status updates. You can check the delivery in the monitor; it shows goods issue posted with a posting date. Inventory impact is immediate. If you check stock for that material right after, it should be reduced by the shipped quantity. And if you navigate to the document flow from the delivery, the goods issue material document is linked there. Nice thing to show in a demo actually; you post goods issue, open the document flow, and you can see the whole chain building up step by step.

**Uma:** And that financial posting has to happen before billing continues?

**Bex:** Correct. After goods issue is posted, the internal sales rep goes back into the sales order and removes the billing block from the final invoice line in the billing plan. Logic is: COGS is recognized, so now it's appropriate to bill the customer for the rest. That block was there to prevent premature billing.

**Uma:** So another manual unblock step?

**Bex:** Same as before; go into the order, billing plan, find the final invoice date line, remove the block, save.

**Uma:** And then the billing clerk creates the final invoice?

**Bex:** Exactly. Same app; Create Billing Documents. This time they pick the final invoice line from the billing due list. Billing type F2, standard invoice. What comes out is the key thing: the final invoice shows the full order amount, but the down payment appears as a deduction; a separate line item. So the customer sees goods value one thousand euros, down payment received minus three hundred euros, amount due seven hundred euros. The financial posting clears the down payment account and posts the remaining receivable.

**Uma:** That deduction has to show correctly on the document?

**Bex:** That's one of the most important acceptance criteria in the whole process. If the down payment doesn't appear as a deduction on the final invoice, the customer gets billed for the full amount. That's wrong. The system handles it automatically through the billing plan and document linkage, but it has to be explicitly verified. The journal entry should also show the down payment liability cleared against the receivable. In productive systems like PR1, PRD, or P10 the account numbers will differ from the demo values; testers should verify the posting logic against whatever chart of accounts is configured, not just copy the example numbers.

**Uma:** And document flow, still intact at this point?

**Bex:** Yes. From the final invoice you should be able to trace all the way back; final invoice, delivery, goods issue, down payment posting, down payment request, sales order. Full chain.

**Uma:** Is there anything at the end; like a reconciliation or monitoring step?

**Bex:** There's the accounts receivable process for posting the incoming final payment; that's technically a separate scope item, J59. And a sales order fulfillment monitoring app, BKK, where you can see order status across the whole cycle. For testing those are usually out of scope for this specific test case, but worth knowing as the natural continuation. The BKJ process itself ends when the final invoice is saved and posted. Everything after is cash collection; finance's territory.

**Uma:** Last question; if you had to pick the two or three things most commonly tested wrong or missed in a test cycle for this, what would those be?

**Bex:** First one is the billing block management. Testers often skip the unblock steps because they're small and easy to miss, then wonder why billing isn't working. Second is the down payment deduction on the final invoice; that's the whole point of the process and it has to be verified explicitly, you can't just assume it worked. Third, honestly, is the document flow. Sounds boring, but a lot of issues in real projects come from broken document chains. You don't check it in testing, you find out in production when someone can't trace a transaction for an audit. Those three cover most of the meaningful risk.

## Acceptance Criteria Summary: Happy Path

_As described by the business expert. Business-level, not technical._

1. **Sales order saves correctly** with billing plan showing two lines: down payment (date category 04) and final invoice (date category 02), with correct amounts.
2. **Only the down payment billing block is removed** after the sales rep's first unblock step; final invoice block remains in place.
3. **Down payment request (FAZ) is created** referencing the sales order, with correct amount and customer.
4. **Financial posting on down payment request** goes to the down payment receivable account (e.g. account 21191000 in demo systems such as BKJ or standard content tenants; verify against chart of accounts in productive systems like PR1, PRD, P10), not revenue.
5. **Delivery cannot be created** before the down payment is posted and cleared. Hard control point.
6. **Delivery creates successfully** when stock is available and payment is confirmed.
7. **Picked quantity matches delivery quantity.** Delivery status reflects picking complete.
8. **Goods issue posts correctly:** inventory reduced, COGS posted, delivery status shows goods issued.
9. **Final invoice billing block is removed** only after goods issue; not before.
10. **Final invoice (F2) shows the down payment as a deduction line item**, net amount due reflects what's still owed.
11. **Financial posting on final invoice** clears the down payment liability and posts the remaining receivable.
12. **Document flow is complete and traceable** end to end: sales order, down payment request, payment, delivery, goods issue, final invoice.

## Edge Cases and Error Scenarios: Short Reference

_The expert flagged these as things testers should check, but didn't go deep on them._

- **No stock at delivery creation:** system should block delivery with a clear message; no partial delivery without explicit configuration.
- **Wrong billing type selected:** if billing clerk chooses F2 instead of FAZ for the down payment step, the billing plan logic breaks; the down payment won't be recognized as a prepayment.
- **Down payment amount exceeds order value:** billing plan setup should prevent this; worth verifying the system validation.
- **Billing block not removed before billing run:** billing due list should not show the item; clerk should get no result or a blocked indicator, not a system error.
- **Goods issue posted but final invoice billing block removal skipped:** final invoice should not be createable; billing due list should not show the final invoice line.
- **Final invoice created without down payment clearance:** this is a configuration gap; the final invoice should always deduct whatever has been posted as a down payment against that sales order. If none is posted, the full amount gets billed; may or may not be valid depending on business rules.
- **Cancellation of down payment request after payment is posted:** the payment needs to be reversed first; the billing document cannot simply be cancelled while an open payment exists against it.
- **Multiple down payments:** the billing plan supports more than one down payment milestone; testers should verify the final invoice correctly deducts all of them, not just the last one.
