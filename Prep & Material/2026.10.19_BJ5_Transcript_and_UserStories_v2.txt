PART 1 – INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Uma:   Marco, good to have you again. So last time we walked through a more
       complete version of this process. Today I want to go through the
       simplified flow we've landed on for the demo. Same idea – just talk
       me through it as if explaining to someone new to production.

Marco: Sure. And yeah, we did simplify. The main thing we cut is the
       material availability check upfront. In the real world that's an
       important gate, but for what we're demonstrating here it adds
       complexity we don't need. We're assuming materials are there. So
       the process starts clean: demand exists, we create the order.

Uma:   Let's start there then. The trigger.

Marco: So something upstream has decided we need to produce. Could be a
       sales order pulling stock, could be a reorder point being hit,
       could be someone just looking at the forecast and making a call.
       Doesn't matter for our purposes. The signal arrives: produce this,
       this quantity, this date. That triggers the first step.

Uma:   Which is manual creation of the production order.

Marco: Right. And we call it "manual" specifically to distinguish it from
       the MRP-driven path where the system creates planned orders and
       you convert them. Here the planner creates the production order
       directly. Same document, same result – it's just the starting
       point that differs. You go into the system, specify the finished
       product, the quantity, the target completion date. The system
       pulls in the bill of materials – all the components you need – and
       assigns a work center, the machine or team that'll do the work.
       That's your production order. The instruction set for the factory.

       At this point the order exists but nothing has happened yet. It's
       not yet visible to the shop floor. That changes in the next step.

Uma:   Release.

Marco: Yes. Release Production Order for Subassembly is what it says in
       our flow – the name comes from the original standard content, we
       couldn't rename it fully, but what it means is simply: release the
       production order. That's the formal green light. The planner has
       reviewed the order and says yes, go ahead. After this, the operator
       can see the order in their worklist and start acting on it.

       Before release: order exists, nobody acts. After release: shop
       floor is authorised to start. That distinction matters for control.
       You don't want people picking materials against an order that might
       still be changed or cancelled.

Uma:   And then picking and goods issue – can you walk through those two
       together? We've grouped them in the flow.

Marco: They go hand in hand, yes. Picking is the physical side: the
       operator goes to the storage location, pulls the components they
       need for the production run, and stages them at the work center.
       All five components, or ten, whatever the BOM says – they gather
       them and bring them to where production is happening.

       Goods issue is the system side of exactly the same event. It's
       the posting that says: these components have left inventory and
       been consumed by this production order. Stock levels drop. The
       cost of those materials moves from the inventory account onto
       the production order. So from a financial and traceability
       standpoint, goods issue is when the components officially stop
       being stock and start being work-in-progress.

       The key thing a tester needs to verify here is the quantity.
       The goods issue quantity has to match the BOM exactly. If it
       doesn't – if someone posts more or less than planned – that's a
       variance. Either the BOM is wrong or something went wrong on the
       floor. Either way it needs to be caught.

Uma:   And then actual production happens, and you confirm.

Marco: Exactly. The operator runs the work – assembles, processes,
       whatever it is – and then confirms the operations. That's how
       we capture what actually happened. You enter the produced quantity,
       the actual time it took at the work center. The system updates
       the order status and relieves the work center capacity.
       That machine or that team is no longer booked against this order.

       And the confirmation is also where scrap gets recorded, if any
       was produced. Some of what you started with didn't make it through.
       That gets noted here, separately from the good quantity.

Uma:   Which leads into the quality gate.

Marco: Right. After confirmation there's a decision point: Quality OK?
       Yes or no. In a full QM setup you'd have a formal inspection
       process, an inspection lot, a usage decision by the quality team.
       In our simplified flow it's a binary gate. Are the goods
       acceptable or not.

       If yes – and that's the happy path – we hand off to the warehouse
       clerk for goods receipt. The finished product arrives in finished
       goods inventory. Done.

       If no – and this is where it gets interesting for the PoC – we
       take the quality failure path.

Uma:   Tell me about that path.

Marco: So the goods haven't passed. First thing that happens is we scrap
       the material. Post Scrap Material. That's a goods issue posting –
       movement type 551 in SAP terms – which removes the output from the
       system. The material that came off the line but failed quality
       gets written off. A material document is created, the cost hits
       the production order as a variance. It's documented, it's auditable.

       Now, having scrapped the output, we've still got a demand to
       fulfil. We needed to produce X units, we produced them, they
       failed. We need more. So the next step on that path is creating
       a purchase requisition for the replacement materials. Maybe
       we need to buy in replacement components that we used up in the
       failed run, or maybe we need to source the finished product from
       a supplier while we investigate what went wrong internally.
       Either way, a PR gets raised.

Uma:   That's a different functional area though – procurement.

Marco: It is. And that's exactly what makes it interesting for the test
       data demonstration. On the happy path you've got production objects –
       production order, material document, goods receipt. On the failure
       path you suddenly need a purchase requisition. Different object,
       different master data requirements. Vendor, purchasing organisation,
       delivery date, price. The test data agent has to reach into a
       different domain to suggest values for those fields. That's a
       richer test.

Uma:   What does the process end state look like on each path?

Marco: Happy path: finished goods in stock. The warehouse clerk has posted
       the goods receipt, the inventory account is up by the produced
       quantity, the production order is technically complete. Downstream
       – sales, shipping – can see the stock and plan deliveries.

       Failure path: goods scrapped, replacement material requested. The
       PR is open, someone in procurement will pick it up and create a
       purchase order. That's outside this process scope. Our process
       ends when the PR exists.

Uma:   Last question – if you had to call out the two most important things
       a tester must get right in this flow, what are they?

Marco: Goods issue quantity – has to match the BOM, zero deviation. If
       that's wrong your inventory is wrong, your costs are wrong, and
       you'll be chasing it for weeks.

       And goods receipt – after confirmation and quality sign-off, the
       finished goods stock must increase by exactly the confirmed
       quantity. Same principle. Zero deviation. And the document flow
       from goods receipt back to the production order must be intact.
       Complete chain, no orphaned documents.

       Everything else is important but those two are where the money is.

Uma:   Clear. Thank you Marco.

Marco: Good luck with it.

PART 2 – USER STORIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Covers the final BJ5_simple flow as published in CALM.
Reduced from 10 to 7 stories. Pick Components and Post Goods Issue merged
into a single execution story. Material availability check removed (step
not in final flow). N-path stories consolidated into one.
Format: Title / Story / Acceptance Criteria / Priority / Story Points
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


US-01  MANUAL CREATION OF PRODUCTION ORDER
Priority: High | Story Points: 3
----------------------------------------
As a Production Planner,
I want to manually create a production order specifying the finished
product, quantity, target date, and work center,
so that the system generates a complete work instruction with all required
component information pulled from the bill of materials.

Acceptance Criteria:
- Production order is created with status "Created"
- BOM is exploded automatically; all required components listed with
  correct quantities and storage locations
- Work center is assigned
- Target completion date is recorded
- Order is not visible in the operator's worklist until released


US-02  RELEASE PRODUCTION ORDER
Priority: High | Story Points: 2
----------------------------------------
As a Production Planner,
I want to release the production order when ready for production,
so that the shop floor operator receives formal authorisation to begin
picking and production.

Acceptance Criteria:
- Production order status changes from "Created" to "Released"
- Released order appears in the production operator's worklist
- System records release date and user
- Order cannot be modified for key fields (quantity, material) after release
  without a specific change procedure


US-03  PICK COMPONENTS AND POST GOODS ISSUE
Priority: High | Story Points: 3
----------------------------------------
As a Production Operator,
I want to pick the required components from storage and post the goods
issue against the production order,
so that components are physically staged at the work center and their
consumption is correctly recorded in inventory and cost accounting.

Acceptance Criteria:
- All components in the BOM can be identified with storage location and
  available quantity
- Goods issue is posted only after the order is released
- Component stock is reduced by exactly the BOM-specified quantity;
  zero deviation from planned consumption
- A material document is created referencing the production order
- Financial posting: component inventory account credited, production
  order debited
- Confirmation step cannot proceed before goods issue is posted


US-04  CONFIRM PRODUCTION OPERATIONS
Priority: High | Story Points: 3
----------------------------------------
As a Production Operator,
I want to confirm completed production operations by recording the
produced quantity and actual operation time,
so that the production order reflects real output and work center
capacity is correctly relieved.

Acceptance Criteria:
- Confirmation is only possible after goods issue is posted
- Actual produced quantity and actual operation time are recorded
- Scrap quantity is separately capturable with a reason
- Work center capacity is relieved by the confirmed time
- Production order status updates to "Confirmed"
- System records confirming user and timestamp


US-05  QUALITY ASSESSMENT GATE
Priority: Medium | Story Points: 2
----------------------------------------
As a Production Planner,
I want a quality decision point after production operations are confirmed,
so that only goods meeting quality standards proceed to goods receipt and
failing goods are routed to the scrap and replenishment path.

Acceptance Criteria:
- Quality decision is available only after confirmation
- "Yes" path: process continues to goods receipt in Warehouse Clerk lane
- "No" path: process routes to Post Scrap Material step; no goods receipt
  is triggered
- Decision is recorded with user and timestamp


US-06  POST GOODS RECEIPT – FINISHED GOODS
Priority: Very High | Story Points: 3
----------------------------------------
As a Warehouse Clerk,
I want to post the goods receipt for the completed production order,
so that finished goods are recorded in inventory and available for
downstream sales and delivery processes.

Acceptance Criteria:
- Goods receipt is posted only after quality assessment passes
- Finished goods stock increases by exactly the confirmed produced quantity;
  zero deviation
- A goods receipt material document is created referencing the production
  order
- Financial posting: finished goods inventory account debited, production
  order credited
- Production order status updates to "Delivered" or "Completed"
- Document flow is fully navigable: goods receipt → confirmation →
  goods issue → production order
- End state: "Finished goods available in stock"


US-07  QUALITY FAILURE PATH: SCRAP AND REPLENISHMENT REQUEST
Priority: Medium | Story Points: 5
----------------------------------------
As a Production Planner,
I want to post the failed output as scrap and raise a purchase requisition
for replacement materials when production fails quality,
so that non-conforming goods are removed from inventory, costs are
correctly captured, and procurement is notified to source replacements.

Acceptance Criteria:
- Post Scrap Material: a goods issue (movement type 551) is posted;
  material stock is reduced by the scrapped quantity; a material document
  is created referencing the production order; cost variance posts to the
  production order
- No goods receipt is posted for scrapped quantities
- Create Purchase Requisition: a PR is created for the replacement material
  or components; PR contains material, quantity, plant, requested delivery
  date, and company code
- PR is created in "Open" status and visible to the purchasing team
- End state: "Goods scrapped. Replacement material requested"
- Document flow links scrap posting and PR to the originating production
  order
